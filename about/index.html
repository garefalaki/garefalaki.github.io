About Me

PhD candidate in Evolutionary Biology @ LMU Munich

I am an evolutionary biologist and computational genomics researcher completing a PhD at LMU Munich and the International Max Planck Research School for Biological Intelligence (IMPRS-BI).

I study biological variation, with a core focus on DNA methylation, quantitative genetics, and the challenge of separating true biological signal from technical variation in genomic data.

Where my mind is heading

My anchor remains in the genome, the epigenome, and the complex space between genotype and phenotype. From that foundation, I am increasingly interested in other forms of variation and in looking at non-biological systems through an evolutionary lens, including AI agents and information ecosystems.

What I communicate

Alongside research, I write and communicate science in Greek and English. Through long-form popular science writing, teaching and public talks, I cover genetics, biodiversity, astronomy, AI and research culture, as well as the social dimensions of science, including identity, conservation, bias and belonging.
