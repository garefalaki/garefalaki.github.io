About

Hi, I’m Ioanna.

I am an evolutionary biologist and computational genomics researcher completing a PhD at LMU Munich and the International Max Planck Research School for Biological Intelligence (IMPRS-BI).

What I study: I investigate biological variation, with a core focus on DNA methylation, quantitative genetics, and the challenge of separating true biological signal from technical variation in genomic data.

Where my mind is heading: My anchor remains firmly in the genome, the epigenome, and the complex space between genotype and phenotype. But from that foundation, I find myself often flirting with non biological systems from an evolutionary scope, from thinking about other forms of variation, inspired by Evolution in Four Dimensions, to the recent boom of AI and AI agents. I am fascinated by viewing all these systems through an evolutionary biologist’s lens.

What I communicate: Alongside my research, I write and communicate science in Greek and English. Through long-form popular science essays, teaching, and public talks, I cover genetics, biodiversity, astronomy, AI, and research culture, as well as the social dimensions of science, including identity, conservation, bias, and belonging.

Selected Research Projects

DNA methylation repeatability and technical variation

LMU Munich · Wolf Lab

Developing statistical approaches to quantify DNA methylation repeatability and distinguish biological variation from technical measurement variation in large-scale epigenomic datasets.

Quantitative genetic modelling of epigenetic variation

LMU Munich · Wolf Lab

Using pedigrees of wild blue tits and large EM-seq/RRBS datasets to investigate whether DNA methylation can be modelled as a quantitative trait and to estimate its heritable component.

Human multi-omics and vaccine response

Biomedical Sciences Research Center “Alexander Fleming”

Analysed transcriptomic and proteomic responses to BNT162b2 vaccination across healthy, elderly and Multiple Myeloma cohorts.

Evolutionary genomics of transposable elements

FORTH / University of Crete

Studied genetic variation and signatures of selection around polymorphic transposable elements in rice using population-genomic analyses and an open-source workflow.
