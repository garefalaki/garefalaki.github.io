About

PhD candidate at LMU Munich and IMPRS-BI, working across evolutionary biology, computational genomics and science communication.

I am an evolutionary biologist and computational genomics researcher studying how much of the variation we measure in biological data is real, repeatable and biologically meaningful.

My current work focuses on DNA methylation, quantitative genetics and technical variation in large epigenomic datasets. I am also increasingly interested in applying evolutionary thinking beyond classical biological systems, including information ecosystems and AI agents.

Alongside research, I write and talk about genetics, biodiversity, astronomy, AI, research culture and the social dimensions of science.

Research

Questions I currently work on, from methylation repeatability to quantitative genetics.

Evolutionary biologyEpigeneticsDNA methylationQuantitative geneticsGenomicsStatistical modellingBioinformatics

DNA methylation repeatability and technical variation

LMU Munich · Wolf Lab

Developing statistical approaches to quantify methylation repeatability and separate biological signal from technical measurement variation.

Quantitative genetics of epigenetic variation

LMU Munich · Wolf Lab

Using wild blue tit pedigrees and large EM-seq/RRBS datasets to study whether DNA methylation can be modelled as a quantitative trait.

Human multi-omics and vaccine response

Biomedical Sciences Research Center “Alexander Fleming”

Analysed transcriptomic and proteomic responses to BNT162b2 vaccination across healthy, elderly and Multiple Myeloma cohorts.

Evolutionary genomics of transposable elements

FORTH / University of Crete

Studied population-genomic variation and signatures of selection around polymorphic transposable elements in rice.
