About

I am a Computational evolutionary biologist studying how (epi)genetic variation shape evolution in natural populations through statistical genomics and bioinformatics. I am finishing my PhD in the Division of Evolutionary Biology at Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich and International Max Planck Research School of Biological Intelligence.

Research Projects

Epigenetic heritability in wild bird populations

Keywords: Quantitative Genetics, DNA Methylation, Parus major

This project investigates to what extent DNA methylation patterns are passed from parents to offspring in wild great tit populations. By utilizing pedigree data and bisulfite sequencing, I quantify the heritability of epigenetic markers and their contribution to phenotypic variance independently of genetic variation.

DNA methylation repeatability framework

Keywords: Statistical Modeling, Biostatistics, R

I developed a robust statistical framework to distinguish biological "noise" from stable epigenetic signals. By analyzing longitudinal samples from the same individuals, this work establishes how much of the observed methylation variation is consistent over time, which is a prerequisite for studying adaptive epigenetic responses.

Multi-omics analysis of vaccine response

Keywords: Bioinformatics, Transcriptomics, Integration

Integrating transcriptomic and methylomic data, this project explores the molecular mechanisms underlying individual variation in vaccine efficacy. We look for specific epigenetic "signatures" that predict how strongly an immune system will react to a stimulus.

Adaptive evolution around transposable elements

Keywords: Evolutionary Genomics, TEs, Population Genetics

Transposable elements (TEs) are often silenced by methylation. This project examines how TE insertions influence nearby gene expression and whether the resulting epigenetic changes provide a "fast-track" for populations to adapt to new environmental pressures.
